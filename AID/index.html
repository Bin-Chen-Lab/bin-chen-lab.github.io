Center for AI-Enabled Drug Discovery (AID)

Democratizing AI-based drug discovery in academia

What We Do

The mission of AID in the College of Human Medicine at Michigan State University (MSU) is to bring together AI, big data, and the local drug discovery community; to train the next generation of scientists with specialized expertise; and ultimately to grow into a sustainable research and education program.

To achieve research excellence, AID leverages emerging big data (e.g., single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, electronic health records), state-of-the-art AI methods (e.g., graph neural networks, transfer learning, deep reinforcement learning), and advanced preclinical models (e.g., ex vivo tissue slice cultures, organoids). AID takes a novel, patient-centric approach: drugs are proposed based on RNA features derived from patients and then validated in models derived from patient tissues.

AID supports both the repurposing of existing drugs for new indications, which can speed up discovery and approval, and the discovery of novel compounds, which takes longer but offers new therapeutic approaches and intellectual property. A key strength of AID is its unique transcriptomics-based drug discovery platform, developed by our team members.
